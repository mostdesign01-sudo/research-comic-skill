# Visual language

The reference comes from a user-authored HTML course history illustration. Extract the visual grammar, not company content.

- Ink #070b0c; panel #0b1112; paper #edf3f2; muted #8d9896; cyan #01c2c3 and #78ffff.
- Black/gray/white dominate. Cyan occupies less than 10%, emphasizing a link, discovery, or selected element.
- Fine engraved contours, crosshatching, stippling, halftone. Avoid noisy pixel filters that erase detail.
- Human-scale artifacts, books, research instruments, fossils and diagrams create narrative. People are optional, never obligatory in every image.
- Use restrained editorial comic panels rather than exaggerated anime faces or superhero effects.
- Match images with editable sans-serif Chinese titles and monospaced numbering. Captions describe the relationship, not everything visible.
- Images preserve natural aspect ratio. Use contain for scientific diagrams; crop only when the full object/relationship survives.
- Motion is optional: slow opacity/transforms, reduced-motion support, no particle systems.
